## Analysis Verdict from Claim Verdicts: `parcialmente falsa` (2026-10-12) — adopted

A fix to the Analysis Verdict, not to the judge. Averaging the falsehood score of the
Claims with Evidence lets a well-supported true Claim offset a false one. With Mistral
Small, the text "drinking diluted bleach kills the virus" + "a paracetamol overdose
causes liver damage" comes out **verdadera**: bleach false (0.67), paracetamol true
(0.17, four `supports`), average 0.42. Another text with refuted homeopathy and two true
Claims, the same. The single-Claim gold set cannot see it: with one Claim, the average
is that Claim.

The Analysis Verdict now follows from each Claim's Verdict (ADR-0002): false and true
Claims together give `parcialmente falsa`, with the Confidence of the strongest false
Claim. Single-Claim results do not change. In `evaluation.evaluate_pipeline`,
`parcialmente falsa` counts as a falsehood warning (positive), like `falsa`. A gold-set
row the extractor splits into two Claims can produce it.

Open: a multi-Claim set (15–20 texts with their key Claims and the expected Analysis
Verdict) to measure this rule and the extractor's trimming.

## Explainer v6: grounded in the abstracts (2026-10-14) — adopted

The explanation does not decide the label, so the gold run cannot see it: the harness
turns it off and, until now, did not store it. Found on a real analysis: «75–90 mg/día
(según la OMS)», a US figure credited to WHO, and an 8%/14% reduction credited to a paper
whose abstract has neither figure. The cause: the explainer received each Source's
**title only**, so every concrete fact came from the model and was pinned on a title.

v6 gives it each shown Source's abstract and the judge's stance per Claim, and the prompt
requires every figure, dose, risk or effect to appear in an abstract, forbids crediting
organisations that are not among the Sources, drops «explicárselo al paciente» and
«exhaustivo», and bans individual dosing or treatment advice.

Method: `EvalRow` now stores `explanation`. A fixed sample of 22 texts (19 gold rows,
stratified by yesterday's verdict and seeded, plus vitamin C and the two mixed texts of
2026-10-12) ran with the explanation on, v5 first and then v6, replaying v5's recorded
Evidence (62/89 searches replayed; the extractor does not always repeat its queries).
Criteria fixed before measuring; figures checked against the abstracts by script and
then by reading every flag.

| Criterion                                         | v5                 | v6       | Target |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------- | ------ |
| Explanations with a figure in no abstract         | 18/22              | 0/22     | 0      |
| Naming OMS, CDC, ECDC, NICE or IARC               | 8/22               | 0/22     | 0      |
| Verdict wording matches the decided verdict       | 20/22              | 21/22    | 22/22  |
| Dosing or individual treatment advice             | 11/22 flagged      | 0/22     | 0      |
| Claims explained                                  | 29/29              | 29/29    | 29/29  |
| Mean length (words)                               | 754                | 572      | —      |

- **v5 invented specifics and credited them to Sources**, not only background facts: «la
  tasa de autismo… 1.04% frente al 1.19% (p = 0.06)» for a cohort that reports a hazard
  ratio; «28 estudios (n = 1245678)… reducción del 62%» for a review of 12; «eficacia del
  93% (CDC)» and «73% (OMS)».
- **v6 residual: a stance restated as a finding.** Of the 16 cited Sources whose abstract
  reports no result, 2 get one: the explainer turns the judge's `contradicts` into «no se
  encuentra evidencia» for a review whose abstract only states its purpose, and gives a
  reason for an `inconclusive` Source that has no abstract at all. Every figure was
  checked; «daño histopatológico en el 100% de los animales», first flagged, is in its
  abstract («100.0% of rats in Group B»).
- **The one mismatch is the judge, not the explainer**: on «vaccines cause autism»,
  decided `verdadera` (the inversion of 2026-10-13), v5 writes «FALSO» and v6
  «INCIERTO… no puede ser validada como verdadera». With the abstracts in hand, the
  explainer refuses the inverted verdict, so the report contradicts its own badge.
- `FDA` in v6 is legitimate: those Sources are openFDA drug labels.

**Found on the way, a Verdict rule gap**: «Beber lejía diluida mata el virus en el
organismo. Una sobredosis de paracetamol causa daño hepático.» comes out `verdadera`
(77.78%) in both runs. No Source was judged relevant to the bleach Claim, so it is
`incierta`; `_overall` ignores uncertain Claims once one is true. The homeopathy text
(homeopathy uncertain, two true Claims) comes out `verdadera` the same way. ADR-0002 made
a true Claim unable to hide a false one; an uncertain one still can.

**v7, rejected.** One more line («la postura… no es un resultado del estudio: si su
resumen no recoge resultados… no le atribuyas ningún hallazgo») on the same 22 texts,
replaying v6's Evidence (59/90). It removed the two stances-as-findings, but filled gaps
from memory elsewhere: «eficacia superior al 95% tras dos dosis» for a Source with no
abstract, «24-48 horas» in an uncertain row, and «consenso científico actual (ej.: OMS,
CDC)». Against the criteria fixed beforehand v6 meets the figure and organisation targets
and v7 does not; a two-row difference either way is within one run's noise.

Open: whether the Analysis Verdict may be `verdadera` while a searched Claim stays
`incierta` (an ADR decision, not a prompt); and Sources with no abstract, which the judge
still gives a stance from the title alone (a measles review `supports`, a scoping review
`inconclusive`), so the explainer has a stance to explain and nothing to ground it in.

The first open item is now ADR-0003 (2026-10-14): an Analysis is `verdadera` only when
every Claim is. Replaying the recorded Stances, the bleach and homeopathy texts become
`incierta`, and so does one correct `verdadera` (chemotherapy side effects).

## Judge v7: finding before stance (2026-10-14) — adopted

v6 already tells the judge, with an example, that a Source showing an effect contradicts a
Claim that denies it, and it still rated agreement with the topic's consensus. So the fix
changes the shape of the answer, not the wording: for each Source the judge first writes
what the Source concludes (`finding`, at most 25 words, written without looking at the
Claim), then its `stance`.

**A set built for the failure.** `data/negations_es.jsonl`: 15 mirrored pairs, each topic
stated affirmatively and negated with opposite labels (8 pairs where the affirmation is
true, 7 where the negation is), on topics outside the gold set. Both halves of a pair
retrieve the same literature, so a judge that reads the consensus gives both the same
Verdict and gets one wrong. Two first-draft pairs were swapped after the baseline because
the judge read their polarity right and the problem was elsewhere: sugar and hyperactivity
(recent reviews do report an association) and antioxidants and lifespan (retrieval
returned mice and flies). Baseline, judge v6: affirmative 15/15, negated 10/15; the 5
errors are the same 5 pairs in two runs, each with both halves given the same Verdict.

**Variants, re-judging recorded Evidence** (`evaluate_judge`-style, all raw candidates):

| Variant                                              | Negated (of 15) | Gold firm right   | Gold falsa→verdadera | Judge failures |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | -------------------- | -------------- |
| v6                                                   | 10              | 64/71 (90.1%)     | 4                    | ~0             |
| A: nested finding + stance                           | 14              | 69/73 (94.5%)     | 3                    | 3/123          |
| C: yes/no question, polarity flipped in code         | 14              | 65/73 (89.0%)     | 7                    | 0              |
| A2: flat fields + "same intervention" rule           | 11/14           | 59/60 (98.3%)     | 1                    | 9/121          |
| A3 = v7: A + all sources, a stance after «sin hallazgo», source count | 13/14 | 69/73 (94.5%) | 3          | 1/123          |

- **C is rejected for its flaw, not its score.** It moves polarity into code, but the
  model filled «what does the Claim answer» from its own knowledge: for «Tomar
  antibióticos… cura el resfriado» it wrote the question right and recorded the Claim's
  answer as «no». The consensus bias moved one field over.
- **A2** abstained more and fixed fewer negations; flat fields failed more often than the
  nested object.
- **A's new errors are loose matches**: a finding about phosphate «supports» a Claim about
  sugar, a detox supplement one about detox juices. A3 kept them (sugar and type 1
  diabetes, detox juices); in the full run below both came out right.
- Judge failures are the model skipping a Source's stance after «sin hallazgo», or
  dropping the last Source of a long answer (myopia, 6 Sources, every time). On a
  malformed answer the judge now asks again once with the Sources split in two halves;
  the myopia case then succeeds 3 of 3. A provider error does not split.
- The examples in v7 use topics in neither set; a draft that reused the antihypertensive
  pair was changed before the full runs.

## Held-out set: the first out-of-sample result (2026-10-14)

50 Spanish health claims never used to build or tune the system, run once on git
`ed7ea47` (judge v7, ADR-0003 rule, Mistral pay-as-you-go, live searches, explanation
off). The set lives outside the repo; only this result is published.

**How it was built.** An AI assistant with no access to the repo collected candidate claims
from published sources, each with its URL and the sentence that settles it: 25 false from
Spanish fact-checkers (Maldita.es, #SaludsinBulos with Spanish medical societies, Newtral)
and 25 true from WHO fact sheets, PAHO, AEMPS and the Ministerio de Sanidad. Topics in the
gold set, the negation pairs and the judge prompt's examples were excluded. Every quote was
then checked against its page (all present), and the rows were read for scope and label:
11 of the first 44 were cut (news events, people, policies, an acronym, one mistranslated
WHO sentence, one label the literature disputes) and replaced by a second batch with a
scope rule. One claim was trimmed to what its source states. The person maintaining the
system read the final claims before the run; nothing that decides a Verdict changed after.

## Judge stance set: measuring loose matching (2026-10-15)

The held-out errors and A3's new errors share one cause: the judge gives `supports` or
`contradicts` to a Source on the same topic that does not test the Claim's point. Verdict
accuracy cannot track it: gold is at 75/76, one loose Source is usually outvoted, and the
held-out set is spent. So it is measured one (Claim, Source) pair at a time.

**The set.** `data/judge_stances_es.jsonl`: 150 pairs over 90 Claims, drawn (seed 7) from
the judge v7 gold and negation runs (`results/gold_judge_v7_2026-10-14.jsonl`,
`results/negations_judge_v7_2026-10-14.jsonl`), stratified by Claim label and v7 stance:
every stance that pointed against its Claim's label (9), 100 other firm stances, 25
`inconclusive` and 16 Sources the judge dropped as `unrelated`, at most two pairs per Claim.
Each pair carries the correct stance and a reason: `on_point`, `background`, `off_point`,
`indirect`, `mixed`, `non_human`, `no_finding` or `other_topic`. It holds no abstracts, so
replaying it needs those two checkpoints, which stay local.

**The rule.** A Source counts when its own finding, or a review's or a drug label's
conclusion, states the Claim's exact point: same exposure, same outcome, same direction.
Sharing the topic does not count, and neither does a point the abstract only implies. An
abstract that states the exact point as established background counts (12 pairs); animal
and lab evidence for a Claim about people is `inconclusive` (4 pairs). Study quality does
not matter: a fringe reanalysis that reports an association still `supports`.

**Two readers.** Both were AI assistants. The first labelled with v7's stances in view; the
second labelled all 150 blind, without those stances, the first labels or the Claims'
labels. They agreed on firm versus not firm in 140/150 (κ = 0.87). Of the 10
disagreements, 5 went to the second reading after a full re-read (the first pass had
skimmed the middle of long abstracts), and the person maintaining the system settled the
other 5 and both rules above. 73 pairs are firm, 77 are not.

**Baseline, judge v7**, three runs re-judging every recorded candidate of the 122
single-Claim samples:


| … of them toward the wrong Verdict       | 6              | 5              | 3              |

- **A third of the judge's firm stances rest on a Source that does not test the Claim**,
  mostly the same topic with another question (`off_point`, 16–19) or an inference the
  abstract does not make (`indirect`, 8–10). Most point the way the Claim's real answer
  goes, which is why Verdict accuracy hides them; the 3–6 that point the other way are the
  held-out errors' mechanism.
- **25 pairs are loose in all three runs**, 18 more in one or two, and 112/150 pairs get the
  same stance in all three. A fix has to cut the 25, read over three runs, with the
  over-strict rate as its guardrail.
- **Not every stance against a label is a judge error.** Of the 9 recorded ones, 4 are right
  readings of Sources that do disagree (the aluminum reanalysis, the USPSTF's small
  multivitamin benefit, the 2004 hepatitis B and multiple sclerosis study, a homeopathy
  meta-analysis). No prompt fixes those.
- Two retrieval issues surfaced: CIMA label text is cut at 2,000 characters, before the
  overdose section, so a label cannot back a Claim about overdose; and in 2 pairs the judge
  gave `supports` from a title with no abstract.
- **The set is in-sample.** It comes from the runs v7 was chosen on; a judge fix tuned on it
  needs a fresh set of Claims for its final number.
